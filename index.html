Welcome, founders.

This toolkit collects the AI prompts and templates you'll use across the semester to build your born-digital, born-global venture. Each stage unlocks here as we reach it in class — check back regularly.

New to AI tools? Start here

Never used an AI chat tool before? Follow these steps exactly.

  1. Open an AI chat tool in your browser. Any of these work and have a free version: chat.openai.com (ChatGPT), claude.ai (Claude), or gemini.google.com (Gemini). You may need to create a free account with your email.
  2. Come back to this page and use the menu on the left to open the step you're working on (for example, "Step A · Idea Generation").
  3. Get the prompt. Click the green Download button to save the prompt as a file, or click Copy to copy the text directly.
  4. Paste it into the message box of the AI chat tool you opened in step 1.
  5. Before you press Enter, replace every bracketed placeholder — things that look like [TEAM NAME / FOUNDER NAMES] — with your team's real information. The AI reads the brackets as instructions, not decoration, so leaving them in will confuse it.
  6. Press Enter (or click the send button) and read the entire response before doing anything else.
  7. Save the AI's answer immediately — copy/paste it into a Google Doc or a plain text file on your computer. Some AI tools do not keep your chat history forever, and you cannot get the output back once it's gone.
  8. Follow the "After you run it" checklist on the step's page to turn that raw AI output into your actual assignment.

Stuck at any point? Ask a classmate or bring it to office hours — this page will not change or grade anything for you, it only gives you the starting prompt.

How to use this

Open the step you're working on, download or copy the prompt block, paste it into the AI tool of your choice, fill in your team's details, and follow the "after you run it" notes to fold the output into your assignment.

Keep your records

Step A — Structured Idea Generation

Use this prompt to generate and screen at least five venture ideas. Do not ask an AI tool for "a good business idea" — this prompt forces constraints, a decision matrix, a competitor check, and pros/cons instead.

The prompt

Act as a startup ideation and market-screening advisor for a three-founder
student team building a born-digital, born-global venture for a semester-long
international business course project.

TEAM CONTEXT
- Founders: [TEAM NAME / FOUNDER NAMES]
- Skills, languages, and relevant experience: [FOUNDER SKILLS, LANGUAGES, PRIOR EXPERIENCE]
- Interest area (leave open if none): [INDUSTRY, INTEREST AREA, OR PROBLEM SPACE]
- Time available to build a working prototype: [WEEKS AVAILABLE TO PROTOTYPE]
- My tool type (has file/code capability, or chat-only): [TOOL TYPE]

HARD CONSTRAINTS — every idea must satisfy all four
1. Digitally deliverable: the core product/service can be delivered entirely
   through software, with no physical shipping required to serve a customer.
2. Potentially scalable across borders: nothing about the idea limits it to one
   country (e.g., no dependence on a single country's physical infrastructure,
   license, or regulator).
3. Solves a definable customer problem: name the customer and the specific pain
   point. Reject ideas where the "problem" is vague or the customer segment is
   "everyone."
4. Feasible for a student team to prototype in the time available: a working
   demo (even a simple one) must be realistic given our skills and weeks above.

TASK 1 — GENERATE IDEAS
Generate at least 5 distinct venture ideas that satisfy all four constraints.
For each idea, provide:
- Working name
- One-sentence pitch
- Target customer (be specific — not "small businesses," but e.g. "solo
  freelance graphic designers in emerging-market cities")
- The customer problem in one sentence
- Why it is digitally deliverable
- Why it is plausible cross-border (which regions/segments might it fit
  beyond one home market, and why)
- Rough revenue model

TASK 2 — SET UP A RESEARCH FOLDER AND ANALYSIS SCRIPT
For each of the 5+ ideas, list 2-3 data points that would help us judge
"global scalability," "customer problem severity," and "data availability" in
the decision matrix in Task 3 (e.g., market size for the target segment,
digital adoption rate, number of visible competitors). For each data point,
name where we could actually find it: our university library databases
(Statista, Euromonitor Passport, IBISWorld, MarketLine, Business Source
Complete) or public sources (World Bank Open Data, ITU DataHub, OECD,
globalEDGE). Do not invent values — only propose what to look for and where.

If my tool type above has file and code capability:
- Create a project folder structure for me: a top-level folder with
  /data/raw (for exported files or pasted tables from library databases),
  /data/sources (a running log: source name, URL/database, date accessed,
  what it's for), and /analysis (scripts and outputs).
- Write a Python script that reads a simple CSV I'll fill in (columns: idea
  name, criterion, raw score 1-5, weight) and outputs: a normalized weighted
  score per idea, a ranked bar chart, and a printed summary table. I will
  fill in the actual scores after I do the research — do not fabricate data
  to fill the template.

If my tool type above is chat-only:
- Give me the same folder structure as a checklist I can create manually in
  Google Drive or on my computer, and give me the same Python script as a
  code block I can run myself in Google Colab once I have real data.

TASK 3 — DECISION MATRIX
Build a decision matrix with the 5+ ideas as rows and these criteria as
columns: customer problem (severity/clarity), global scalability,
differentiation, feasibility for our team, data availability, and founder
interest. Leave every score cell blank for us to fill in after research —
do not assign scores yourself. Suggest a starting weight (must total 100%)
for each criterion and explain the logic for each weight in one sentence.

TASK 4 — COMPETITOR CHECK
For each idea, name 2-3 obvious existing competitors or substitutes (real
products/companies, not hypothetical ones) that already address a similar
problem. For each, state in one or two sentences what would be genuinely
different about our offering. If you are not confident a named competitor is
real and current, say so explicitly instead of guessing.

TASK 5 — PROS AND CONS
For each of the 5+ ideas, list 3-4 pros and 3-4 cons, covering at minimum:
market/customer risk, technical/build risk, and competitive risk.

OUTPUT RULES
- Do not pick a winner. Present all ideas neutrally with their matrix,
  competitor notes, and pros/cons, then stop. The founders — not the AI —
  make the final selection, and that decision and its reasoning will be
  recorded separately.
- Flag anywhere you are uncertain, guessing, or lack current information
  instead of filling the gap with an invented fact.
- Format the response with clear headers per idea plus one combined table
  for the decision matrix.

After you run it

  1. Save the raw output and log it in the AI Use & Founder Decision Log (tool used, prompt summary, what you kept/changed).
  2. Do the actual research named in Task 2 using UA library databases and the public sources listed, and record each source in the Evidence Ledger.
  3. Fill in the decision matrix with real scores tied to that evidence — not the AI's guesses.
  4. As a team, discuss and select one idea. Write down why, in your own words. This decision must be attributed to the founders.
  5. Move to naming the company and checking domain/social availability, then on to Step B.

Step B — Build the AI Market Intelligence Agent/Skill

Use this prompt to have an AI tool build you a reusable Skill — not a one-off answer — that screens candidate countries against seven required criteria, cites its sources, and can be re-run on demand for at least five dated monitoring runs before Assignment 1 is due.

Which AI tool do I need?

Claude Code is recommended for this step. Step A only needed a chat window because you were pasting the AI's answer straight into a document. Step B is different: you're asking the AI to actually build and save a small folder of files (a "Skill") on your computer — instructions, spreadsheet templates, and a script — that your team will reuse for five separate research sessions. That requires a tool that can create and save real files, which a plain chat window (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini in a browser tab) cannot do on its own.

If your team doesn't have access to Claude Code, that's still fine — the prompt below has a built-in fallback: it tells the AI tool to give you the same file contents as copy-pasteable text blocks instead. It'll take a bit more manual copying and saving on your end, but you'll end up with the same Skill.

WHAT TO BUILD
If my tool type has file and skill-creation capability:
Create a Skill (not an autonomous multi-step agent) at `skills/market-intel/`
containing:
1. `SKILL.md` — the reusable instruction set below, filled in with my venture
   context above, governing every future run of this Skill.
2. `templates/evidence-table.csv` — empty template, columns: indicator,
   value, year/date, country, source, data-quality note.
3. `templates/scorecard.csv` — empty template, columns: country, criterion,
   weight, raw score (1-5), weighted score, evidence reference.
4. `scripts/score_markets.py` — reads a filled-in scorecard.csv and outputs a
   weighted total per country, a ranked table, and a ranked bar chart. It
   must only aggregate what's in the file — never invent or estimate a score.
5. `logs/change_log.md` — empty template, columns: run date,
   indicator/country changed, old value, new value, affects ranking
   (yes/no/unclear), founder note.
6. `logs/founder_decision_log.md` — empty template, columns: run date, AI
   recommendation, founder decision (accepted/rejected/modified), reason.
7. `README.md` inside the skill folder explaining how to re-invoke the Skill
   for a new monitoring run and where each output file goes.

If my tool type is chat-only:
Give me the same file contents as labeled code blocks I can save myself, plus
the SKILL.md as a single reusable prompt I can paste at the start of every
new monitoring-run conversation.

SKILL.md CONTENT — fill in my bracketed context above, then use this as the
literal governing instructions:

  Act as our Global Market Intelligence Analyst for [VENTURE NAME]. Use only
  the evidence sources listed above, or sources you can open and verify
  live. For every factual or quantitative claim, return the source and
  year/date. Never invent a missing number, statistic, or citation — if you
  cannot find or verify a value, write "not found / needs founder research"
  instead of estimating it.

  Apply the stated market-screening weights exactly and show the calculation
  for every country (score x weight, summed to a total). No hidden scoring
  logic.

  Every response must follow this exact output structure, in this order:
  1. Evidence snapshot — one table per country: indicator, value, year/date,
     source, data-quality note (current / dated / estimate / unverifiable).
  2. Screening score — weighted score by criterion and overall score per
     country, arithmetic shown.
  3. Opportunity interpretation — clearly labeled "AI INTERPRETATION," never
     mixed into the evidence table above.
  4. Risks/unknowns — missing data, contradictions, legal uncertainty, weak
     proxies, or data older than 3 years.
  5. Recommended next action — what the founders should investigate next,
     phrased as a recommendation, not an instruction to act.
  6. Change since prior run — compare this run's evidence table to the most
     recent entry in logs/change_log.md (or state "first run" if none
     exists), and flag only changes that could plausibly move the ranking.
  7. Founder decision — leave this field BLANK. It is filled in by the
     founders, never by the AI.

  Freshness rule: always show the data's year. Flag anything older than 3
  years as dated, even if it's the newest figure available.

  Evidence rule: SOURCE FACTS and AI INTERPRETATION must never be mixed in
  the same sentence or table cell.

  Founder decision rule: recommend, do not decide. Never select a top market
  for the founders. End every run by prompting them to fill in the Founder
  decision log.

AFTER YOU BUILD IT
Explain in 3-5 sentences how to actually re-run this Skill for a new
monitoring pass — what to paste in, what changes each time, and where each
run's output should be saved — so the team can produce at least five dated
monitoring runs before the assignment is due.

After you run it

  1. Save what your AI tool builds (the whole skills/market-intel/ folder) into your team's project repository and screenshot the folder structure for your report's Section 5.
  2. Do your first real run: fill in templates/evidence-table.csv and templates/scorecard.csv with real data from the permitted sources, then invoke the Skill. Log this run in the AI Use & Founder Decision Log and the Evidence Ledger.
  3. Fill in logs/founder_decision_log.md after every run — record what you accepted, rejected, or changed, and why, in your own words. The AI never fills this in for you.
  4. Re-run the Skill on at least four more separate days before the assignment is due (five dated runs minimum). Each run should update logs/change_log.md with anything that changed since the prior run.
  5. Use the resulting evidence table, scorecard, and change log directly in your report's Section 4 (Country Screening Model and Results) and Section 6 (Monitoring Results).

Step B — the Skill's first run

PitchBridge AI's Skill screened six candidate countries (Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, India, Spain, Canada) against the seven required criteria, using only live public data — every figure sourced and dated, nothing invented.

Ranked: Canada (3.36) > Spain (2.99) > India (2.72) > Mexico (2.16) > Indonesia (2.09) > Brazil (1.88).

This does not match the project guide's illustrative example (which shortlists Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico) — on purpose. The guide's numbers are labeled placeholders; this run used real data and a disclosed scoring method, and a general-purpose scorecard structurally favors large, wealthy economies. The Skill flagged this itself, in its own Risks/Unknowns section, rather than presenting the ranking as clean:

  • Total population is a weak, generic proxy for "market size" — PitchBridge sells to founders and small-business owners, not to the general population, so it overweights India.
  • "Consumption capacity" was an AI-derived estimate, not an independently published figure, and it duplicated the infrastructure criterion instead of measuring something distinct.
  • Country risk used only one governance sub-indicator, omitting inflation/FX volatility and rule of law.
  • India's internet-use figure was a year older than the other five countries' data.
  • No paywalled library-database evidence (Statista, Euromonitor, etc.) was included yet — only public sources.

The Skill's own recommended next action: replace the population-based proxy with a venture-specific one (SME/startup counts, accelerator density), broaden the country-risk measure, and add at least one library-database data point per shortlisted country — before this feeds into Step C. The founders, not the AI, decide what to do with that.

What this worked example is trying to show you

The point isn't the ranking — it's that a careful AI run cites everything, shows its arithmetic, and actively argues against trusting its own weakest assumptions. If your own Step A or Step B output doesn't do that (real sources, dated evidence, explicit "here's what's shaky about this" flags), that's a sign to push your AI tool harder, not a sign to accept what it gave you.
