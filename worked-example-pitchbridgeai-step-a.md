## Task 1 — Five venture ideas

All five sit inside the interest area given (AI role-play coaching for high-stakes
business conversations) but target different customers and moments, so they can
genuinely be compared rather than being five names for the same idea.

### 1. PitchBridge Investor Coach
- **Pitch:** An AI role-play partner that grills early-stage founders with realistic investor Q&A and gives structured feedback on clarity and objection-handling.
- **Target customer:** Solo and early-stage startup founders preparing for accelerator or angel-investor pitches, especially non-native English speakers pitching to English-speaking investors.
- **Customer problem:** Founders freeze or ramble under tough, unscripted investor questions and have no safe, repeatable way to practice.
- **Why digitally deliverable:** Entirely a browser/mobile chat or voice interface; no physical component.
- **Why cross-border:** Investor pitching conventions and English-language fluency gaps are a common friction point for founders in Latin America, Southeast Asia, and Africa raising from Western investors — the product travels with the founder, not with a country's infrastructure.
- **Rough revenue model:** Freemium (limited sessions/month) with a paid tier for accelerator cohorts and unlimited sessions; possible B2B2C licensing to accelerators/incubators.

### 2. SalesFloor AI
- **Pitch:** An AI buyer-persona simulator that lets small-business sales reps rehearse cold and discovery calls against different "toughest customer" types before the real call.
- **Target customer:** Solo entrepreneurs and small B2B/e-commerce sales teams (2-10 people) without a dedicated sales-enablement budget.
- **Customer problem:** Reps lose winnable deals because they haven't rehearsed objection handling and only get real practice in front of paying prospects.
- **Why digitally deliverable:** Chat or voice roleplay app; no physical goods.
- **Why cross-border:** Buyer objection patterns can be localized per market (price sensitivity, decision-maker hierarchy, negotiation norms), letting the same core product serve sellers targeting different regions.
- **Rough revenue model:** Per-seat monthly subscription; usage-based add-on for voice-call simulation minutes.

### 3. CrossCultural Pitch Trainer
- **Pitch:** An AI coach that takes one pitch and rehearses it against culturally distinct investor/buyer personas, flagging where directness, risk framing, or relationship-building norms need to shift by market.
- **Target customer:** Founders explicitly planning to raise from or sell into more than one country/region this year.
- **Customer problem:** Founders don't know how a pitch that works with U.S. investors can misfire with, say, Japanese or German counterparts, and find out the hard way in a real meeting.
- **Why digitally deliverable:** Chat/voice roleplay app layered with a cultural-dimensions framework; no physical component.
- **Why cross-border:** Cross-border adaptation is the entire premise — the product's value increases, not decreases, as more countries are added.
- **Rough revenue model:** Per-pitch or per-market "adaptation pass" pricing, plus a subscription for founders running multiple fundraising or sales campaigns at once.

### 4. CustomerConvo Coach
- **Pitch:** An AI role-play tool for founders handling their own customer support, rehearsing churn-save, price-objection, and complaint-de-escalation conversations before they happen for real.
- **Target customer:** Early-stage SaaS/subscription founders who are still doing their own customer support and don't yet have a trained CS team.
- **Customer problem:** Founders lose recoverable customers because they handle high-stakes retention conversations live, with no training and no budget for one.
- **Why digitally deliverable:** Chat/voice roleplay app; no physical component.
- **Why cross-border:** Retention-conversation norms (how directly to discuss cancellation, appropriate tone) vary by market, and the tool can localize scripts the same way it does for founders elsewhere.
- **Rough revenue model:** Low-cost monthly subscription positioned as "cheaper than losing one customer," scaling with number of support seats.

### 5. Demo Day Dry-Run
- **Pitch:** A narrowly scoped tool that simulates a live, time-boxed Demo Day Q&A session, scoring founders on conciseness and confidence right before an actual pitch competition or demo day.
- **Target customer:** Founders in an accelerator/incubator cohort with a specific, dated demo day on the calendar.
- **Customer problem:** Founders over-rehearse the scripted pitch itself but under-rehearse live, rapid-fire Q&A under time pressure, which is where real demo days are actually won or lost.
- **Why digitally deliverable:** Chat/voice roleplay app with a countdown-timer format; no physical component.
- **Why cross-border:** Sellable directly to accelerators and university entrepreneurship programs worldwide, independent of any one country's demo day calendar.
- **Rough revenue model:** B2B licensing to accelerators/university programs per cohort, plus an individual founder tier.

## Task 5 — Pros and cons

**PitchBridge Investor Coach**
- Pros: large and growing pool of accelerator-affiliated founders; clear, high-stakes problem; several real competitors prove market demand exists.
- Cons: crowded category (three close competitors found); investors' actual questioning style is hard to simulate convincingly; founders may not pay until they've already lost a real pitch once.

**SalesFloor AI**
- Pros: large addressable market (every small business that sells); recurring-revenue-friendly (per-seat pricing); competitors validate demand.
- Cons: incumbents (Hyperbound, Nooks) already serve larger sales orgs and could move downmarket; solo sellers may be price-sensitive; realistic voice-based roleplay is technically harder to build well than text chat.

**CrossCultural Pitch Trainer**
- Pros: strong thematic fit with a "born-global" course and with the founder's own international-business background; the possible market gap (no direct competitor found) is a real opportunity signal, not just an assumption.
- Cons: the "possible gap" could instead mean the two categories don't combine well commercially; cultural-adaptation guidance is hard to make evidence-based rather than stereotyped; smaller, more specialized target audience than the other four ideas.

**CustomerConvo Coach**
- Pros: churn is expensive, so the value proposition is easy to quantify for customers; competitors validate the broader category; underserved niche (founder-as-support-rep) within a proven category.
- Cons: buyers may not prioritize this until they've already lost several customers; three real competitors are already circling this exact use case; harder to reach this specific customer (early-stage founder doing their own support) with targeted marketing.

**Demo Day Dry-Run**
- Pros: very specific, dated urgency (a real deadline) tends to drive faster purchase decisions; low build complexity — a timed Q&A format is simpler than an always-on coaching product; clean B2B channel through accelerators/university programs.
- Cons: usage is inherently one-off/seasonal around demo-day dates, which is a harder revenue model to sustain; overlaps heavily with PitchBridge Investor Coach — may cannibalize rather than complement it; small total number of accelerator programs limits the B2B channel's ceiling.
